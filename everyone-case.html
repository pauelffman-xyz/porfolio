Overview & Challenge
A catalog of brands.
One coherent voice.

Everyone is a multi-brand retailer selling footwear, apparel and accessories from Nike, Adidas, Columbia, CAT, Under Armour, Hush Puppies and Victoria's Secret. The scenario: a two-person design team — myself as Senior Product Designer and a Jr Designer — hired to redesign an outdated site that didn't reflect the brand or meet the needs of a diverse customer base.

Before opening Figma, I framed the brief as three open questions to the client: what does the current brand look like, what are the real user pains today, and what do the current conversion metrics say. Design starts with the right questions, not the first screen.

Project Vision & Strategy
Three main goals
UX · Conversion · Loyalty
Goal 01
Improve the User Experience
Design an intuitive, easy-to-navigate website with an efficient category structure and filters that let users find products fast.
Goal 02
Increase Conversions
Optimise the checkout process to reduce cart abandonment, simplifying steps and improving product information.
Goal 03
Reinforce Brand Loyalty
Convey trust and professionalism through modern design, excellent support and a transparent return policy.
Key elements of the redesign
Element 01
Modern & Attractive Design
Update the aesthetics to reflect the modernity and identity of Everyone, with a clean design and fonts that appeal to the target audience.
Element 02
Mobile Optimisation
A coherent version that looks amazing on mobile — since 70% of sales happen there.
Element 03
Intuitive Navigation
Revised flows and navigation accessible to all, with limited, simple steps. Filter section rethought from scratch.
Element 04
Detailed Product Content
Detailed descriptions, high-quality images and customer reviews to improve trust and purchasing decisions.
Element 05
Enhanced Customer Support
Live chat, email assistance and a comprehensive FAQ section to resolve queries efficiently.
Element 06
Security & Confidence
Security seals and visible trust certifications so users feel their data is protected during transactions.

Process · Two-Week Sprint
Daily syncs.
Clear ownership.

With only 17 hours of hands-on time, the team ran a tight cadence: a kickoff meeting to assign tasks, a 10:30am daily standup to unblock and re-prioritise, and two clearly scoped weeks — Research, then Ideation & Validation.

Week 01 · Research
Data, survey & flows
PM collects & analyses existing site data and runs a survey on shopping pains. Designers map current user flows and run a competitive benchmark. Results are synthesised into a report ahead of the client sync.
Week 02 · Ideation & Validation
Wireframes → UI → Prototype → Test
Wireframes and design schemes based on defined flows, then visual design (colour, typography, graphic style) and the main screens: Home, shopping flow, Login + onboarding. Testing script built for checkout and filters; insights fold back into iteration before the MVP ships.

Learnings
Learning 01
Discovery questions beat assumptions
Framing the brief as branding, UX and metrics questions before designing surfaced real constraints the team would have otherwise guessed at.
Learning 02
Leading a Jr Designer is a design skill too
Distributing tasks by strength, running quality checks and supporting growth took as much intention as any screen decision.
Learning 03
Mobile-first isn't optional at 70%
With the majority of sales on mobile, every desktop decision was cross-checked against how it would collapse — filters especially.
The core principle
"A multi-brand catalog only feels premium when the layer holding it together is invisible."
Nike, Adidas, Under Armour and Victoria's Secret each carry their own visual language. Everyone's job was never to compete with that — it was to give all of it one calm, trustworthy frame.
